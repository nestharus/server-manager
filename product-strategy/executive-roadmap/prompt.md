# Executive Roadmap: Translate Product Strategy into Business Strategy

## Your Role

**You are a business strategist writing for executive leadership. Do NOT reference code, files, endpoints, or technical implementation details. Your output is a business-facing executive roadmap only.**

Take the product expansion roadmap (which was built from a codebase audit) and translate it into a document that leadership can use to make investment, hiring, and go-to-market decisions.

## Provided Files

- **`roadmap.md`** — A value-prioritized product expansion roadmap.
- **`proposal.md`** — The system design proposal.
- **`problem.md`** — The problem definition.

## What To Do

### 1. Market context and opportunity sizing

Open with why this market exists and why now. Include:
- Quantified pain points users face daily
- Why existing tools fail this segment
- Concrete examples of current user struggles

### 2. Current product strengths (business language)

Translate the technical capability map from `roadmap.md` into business value statements. Do not mention databases, APIs, routers, or code.

### 3. Expansion roadmap (business case per priority)

For each priority from `roadmap.md`, write an executive-facing entry:
- **What it does** — one paragraph, plain language
- **Who it helps** — the specific user persona and their current pain
- **Business value** — why this matters
- **Effort / Impact / Timeline** — simple table (Low/Medium/High)

### 4. Quick wins

Present quick wins as a table with customer impact and business justification.

### 5. What we are NOT building (and why)

Present deferred items as deliberate strategic choices, not omissions.

### 6. Competitive positioning

How the product wins on the dimensions that matter to target users.

### 7. Recommended next steps

Close with a clear action plan: top 3 priorities with investment level, timeline, and expected outcome.

## Writing Guidelines

- **No technical language.** No code, no file paths, no API references.
- **Quantify wherever possible.**
- **Write for a reader who makes investment decisions.**
- **Be direct and opinionated.**
- **Use tables for comparisons and summaries.**
- **Keep total length under 3,500 words.**
